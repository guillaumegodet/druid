:::note[In short]
- **Goal:** measure the co-publications of the institution (or of a laboratory) with the
  universities of a European alliance, identify the driving researchers and topics, and
  produce presentation material.
- **For:** international relations, research office, central services; a laboratory's
  management for its own laboratory.
- **Time:** one to two hours.
- **Example used:** the EUniWell alliance, available as a predefined group. The walkthrough is
  the same for any group of institutions.
:::

## Step 1 — Choose the structure and the period

1. Open the **Dashboard**.
2. Choose the **structure**: the institution for an overall review, a laboratory for a
   targeted one.
3. Set the **period**: five years usually give a readable trend.
4. Leave the scope on **Affiliation**.

## Step 2 — Build the group of partners

1. Open **Collaborations › Partner institutions**.
2. Click the predefined **EUniWell** group: its member universities are added.
3. For another alliance, search for each institution by name and add it; only the
   institutions the structure has co-published with are suggested.

## Step 3 — Leave out very large collaborations

Tick **Exclude large collaborations**. Publications with several hundred authors (particle
physics, large consortia) inflate the figures a lot without reflecting a lab-to-lab
collaboration. State in your review whether you excluded them.

## Step 4 — Read the review

| Item | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Number of co-publications and yearly trend | Volume and trend. |
| **By university** | Co-publications with each member; click a bar to see the publications. |
| The institution's researchers involved | The people behind these collaborations, with their laboratory. |
| Fields and subfields | The topics of the co-publications. |
| Recent publications | Concrete examples to illustrate. |

:::caution[Reading the figures]
A publication co-signed with several universities of the alliance counts **for each** in
**By university**: the sum of the bars exceeds the total. For an overall figure, take the
number of co-publications with the group.
:::

## Step 5 — Explore a topic in depth

*If your instance offers thematic analysis.*

1. In **Thematic analysis (AI)**, describe the topic you are interested in (“health and
   well-being”, “energy transition”…).
2. Check the suggested subjects: remove those that do not fit, **add a subject** if needed.
3. **Generate the analysis**: volume, trend, fields and researchers for that subject only.

## Step 6 — Check and export

1. **See the publications** of a bar or of the group: check a few publications (an
   affiliation badly recognised by OpenAlex can add or remove a partner).
2. Export:
   - the charts as **PNG** for a slide show;
   - the list of publications as **CSV** from the list;
   - a complete report: in **My reports › New report**, the **Collaboration with a university
     or group** template, on the same partners, brings together key figures, an impact
     comparison, an analysis by theme and the list of co-publications (see
     [Report templates](/en/guides/rapports/modeles/#collaboration-with-a-university-or-group)).
3. **Copy link** to share the view with a colleague who has access to Druid.
