The **Collaborations** tab shows who the structure publishes with: the institution's other
laboratories, French institutions, foreign partners, and university alliances. It reads on
the period and scope chosen at the top of the dashboard.

## The sub-tabs

| Sub-tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Typology** | The share of each type of collaboration and its yearly trend. |
| **Within the structure** | For a composite structure: co-publications between its internal laboratories. |
| **(name of your institution)** | The institution's other laboratories that co-sign the most, and the shared topics. |
| **National** | The French institutions (outside the institution) that co-sign the most. |
| **International** | Foreign countries and organisations: map, ranking, trends, flows. |
| **By country** | The collaboration with **one** partner country: its institutions, the labs and researchers involved, themes, impact, funders. |
| **Partner institutions** | The review of co-publications with a chosen institution or group of institutions. |

Collaboration types are defined in
[Publications and attribution](/en/donnees/publications-attribution/#collaborations). A
publication can combine several; the **Typology** keeps the broadest category for each.

## Review collaborations with a country

1. Open the **By country** sub-tab.
2. Choose the **Partner country**: countries are listed alphabetically, with their number of
   co-publications over the period; type a few letters to filter the list (accents ignored,
   French or English name).
3. If needed, tick:
   - **Exclude large collaborations (> 50 authors)** to leave out large consortia everywhere;
   - **Group hospitals and institutes with their university** to count, for example, a heart
     institute with the university it depends on.
4. Read the blocks, from top to bottom:
   - **key figures**: co-publications, share of the international co-publications, rank of the
     country among the partner countries, trend, share of **bilateral** co-publications (no
     other foreign country), researchers and labs involved, open access, large collaborations;
   - **yearly trend** and **rank** of the country among the partner countries;
   - **institutions of the country**: map, ranking and, when the export knows them, breakdown
     by **province, state or region**;
   - **labs and researchers involved**, and **Who works with whom**: the main labs crossed with
     the main institutions of the country;
   - **themes**: fields, subfields and **specialization** (see below);
   - **impact**, compared with a comparable reference;
   - **funding**: the funders acknowledged, depending on whether they are from the country,
     European, French or elsewhere;
   - **other countries and languages**: bilateral share, other countries of the multilateral
     co-publications, languages.
5. Click an institution, a researcher, a cell of the cross table, a funder or a subfield to open
   the matching publications; **View the N publications** opens the whole list.

The page link keeps the country and the ticked options: you can share it as is. Each chart can
also be **added to a report** with its country; for a full report, use the
[Collaboration with a country](/en/guides/rapports/modeles/#collaboration-with-a-country)
template.

### Reading the specialization

The **specialization** index compares the weight of a subfield in the co-publications with the
country with its weight in **all** the international co-publications of the structure. Above 1,
the subfield is more present with this country than with the others; below 1, less. Only
frequent enough subfields are kept (20 co-publications, 5 for a small corpus).

### Reading the impact

The impact of the co-publications (median FWCI, top 10% share) is compared with the **other**
international co-publications of the structure in the same subfields, weighted like the
co-publications with the country. Publications with more than 50 authors are always left out.

## Review collaborations with an institution or an alliance

1. Open the **Partner institutions** sub-tab.
2. Choose a **predefined group** (for example an alliance of European universities such as
   EUniWell), or search for a partner institution by name. You can add several to build your
   own group.
3. Read the review: number of co-publications, yearly trend, fields and subfields, the
   institution's most involved researchers.
4. For a group of universities, **By university** details the co-publications with each
   member; click a bar to see the publications.
5. Tick **Exclude large collaborations** to leave out publications with a very large number
   of authors (particle physics, large consortia), which inflate the figures.

### Thematic analysis

On some instances, **Thematic analysis (AI)** lets you describe a topic (“maritime
decarbonisation”, “AI and health”…): the assistant suggests the matching subjects among
those actually present in the co-publications. Correct the list, then **Generate the
analysis**.

## What happens next

Nothing is changed. Each chart can be exported or shared (see
[Export data and reports](/en/guides/tableau-de-bord/exporter/)), and the detailed
publications opened in the list.

## Known limitations

- **A publication co-signed by several partners counts for each of them**: the sum of the
  bars exceeds the total of co-publications.
- **Institutions come from OpenAlex**: a badly recognised affiliation makes a partner
  disappear (or appear wrongly).
- **Co-affiliations of the same author** (a researcher attached to both the university and
  the CNRS) are not counted as collaborations.
- **Thematic analysis** is an aid: the suggested subjects need checking.
- **By country**: a publication belongs to the country when one of its authors is affiliated
  with an institution of that country. Province and region names are OpenAlex's, in English. For
  an institution export, clicking a lab does not open the list (labs are carried by the
  authors there, not by the publications).
- **Funders are those the publications acknowledge**: they say neither who paid the research
  nor who holds the grant. The European Commission has no country: it is classed « Europe ».
