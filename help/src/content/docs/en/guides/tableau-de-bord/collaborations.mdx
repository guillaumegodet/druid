The **Collaborations** tab shows who the structure publishes with: the institution's other
laboratories, French institutions, foreign partners, and university alliances. It reads on
the period and scope chosen at the top of the dashboard.

## The sub-tabs

| Sub-tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Typology** | The share of each type of collaboration and its yearly trend. |
| **Within the structure** | For a composite structure: co-publications between its internal laboratories. |
| **(name of your institution)** | The institution's other laboratories that co-sign the most, and the shared topics. |
| **National** | The French institutions (outside the institution) that co-sign the most. |
| **International** | Foreign countries and organisations: map, ranking, trends, flows. |
| **Partner institutions** | The review of co-publications with a chosen institution or group of institutions. |

Collaboration types are defined in
[Publications and attribution](/en/donnees/publications-attribution/#collaborations). A
publication can combine several; the **Typology** keeps the broadest category for each.

## Review collaborations with an institution or an alliance

1. Open the **Partner institutions** sub-tab.
2. Choose a **predefined group** (for example an alliance of European universities such as
   EUniWell), or search for a partner institution by name. You can add several to build your
   own group.
3. Read the review: number of co-publications, yearly trend, fields and subfields, the
   institution's most involved researchers.
4. For a group of universities, **By university** details the co-publications with each
   member; click a bar to see the publications.
5. Tick **Exclude large collaborations** to leave out publications with a very large number
   of authors (particle physics, large consortia), which inflate the figures.

### Thematic analysis

On some instances, **Thematic analysis (AI)** lets you describe a topic (“maritime
decarbonisation”, “AI and health”…): the assistant suggests the matching subjects among
those actually present in the co-publications. Correct the list, then **Generate the
analysis**.

## What happens next

Nothing is changed. Each chart can be exported or shared (see
[Export data and reports](/en/guides/tableau-de-bord/exporter/)), and the detailed
publications opened in the list.

## Known limitations

- **A publication co-signed by several partners counts for each of them**: the sum of the
  bars exceeds the total of co-publications.
- **Institutions come from OpenAlex**: a badly recognised affiliation makes a partner
  disappear (or appear wrongly).
- **Co-affiliations of the same author** (a researcher attached to both the university and
  the CNRS) are not counted as collaborations.
- **Thematic analysis** is an aid: the suggested subjects need checking.
