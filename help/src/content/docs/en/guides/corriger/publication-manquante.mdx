A publication you expected does not appear in the dashboard, or a publication that has
nothing to do with the structure shows up there. Here is how to find the cause, starting with
the most frequent ones.

## A publication is missing

### 1. Check the settings

In the dashboard:

1. does the **period** cover the year of the publication?
2. switch to the **Affiliation** scope: the *Headcount* scope leaves out publications none of
   whose authors is recognised among the members;
3. in **Publication list**, search for the title or the DOI, **without any filter**.

If it appears under *Affiliation* but not under *Headcount*, go to step 4.

### 2. Check whether the publication is recent

A publication less than a few weeks old may not be in OpenAlex or the French Open Science
Monitor yet. And the dashboard is only updated when it is **regenerated**. Wait, or ask
[support](/en/guides/support/) for a regeneration.

### 3. Check the signed affiliation

A publication enters a structure's corpus if **an author signed with its affiliation**. Open
the publication (at the publisher's, in HAL or on openalex.org) and look at the affiliation
written by the authors:

- **the affiliation does not mention the structure** (only the university, or another
  supervising body): the publication cannot be found through the affiliation. Deposit it in
  HAL with the right affiliation, or check that the author has their identifiers in Druid
  (see step 5);
- **the affiliation is correct but misread by OpenAlex**: see
  [An affiliation is wrong](/en/guides/corriger/affiliation-fausse/).

### 4. Check the author (Headcount scope)

If the publication is present under *Affiliation* but not under *Headcount*, its author is
not recognised as a member:

- their **record is not validated**, or they are **leaving**: only validated records count
  as members;
- they have **left**: the *Headcount* scope relies on current members;
- their **name** in the publication differs too much from the one on the record (name in
  use, abbreviated compound name).

Correct the record (see [Correct a person's status or affiliation](/en/guides/personnes/fiabiliser-statut-rattachement/));
the publication will be recognised at the next regeneration.

### 5. Check the identifiers (CRISalid harvesting)

If your institution uses CRISalid, a researcher's publications are also harvested from their
**identifiers** (IdRef, ORCID, IdHAL, OpenAlex, Scopus). A person without identifiers is
only found through the affiliation. Complete their identifiers (see
[Align a lab's identifiers](/en/guides/identifiants/aligner-les-identifiants/)). The
dashboard's **Sources** tab shows what CRISalid finds compared with the other sources.

## A publication is wrongly attributed

A publication unrelated to the structure appears in its dashboard. Possible causes:

- **an affiliation wrongly assigned by OpenAlex**: the author signs elsewhere, but OpenAlex
  linked their affiliation to your structure. See
  [An affiliation is wrong](/en/guides/corriger/affiliation-fausse/);
- **a namesake** wrongly recognised as a member (same surname, same initial): visible in the
  tabs by team or by researcher;
- **a federation or a laboratory with several supervising bodies** linked in OpenAlex to your
  institution: the technical team can exclude the institution concerned. Report it to
  [support](/en/guides/support/) with the link to the publication.

## What happens next

Corrections made in Druid (records, identifiers) or at the source (HAL, OpenAlex) only show
in the dashboard at its **next regeneration**. Corrections in OpenAlex can take several
weeks.

## Known limitations

- **Publications without a DOI** (books, chapters, conference papers) are less well covered:
  HAL is often the only source that knows them.
- **A publication signed without the structure's name** cannot be attributed to it through
  the affiliation, whatever the source.
- **The Headcount scope ignores former members**: for an evaluation report, use the
  *Affiliation* scope.
