The **Researcher pairs** sub-tab of the **Partnerships** view lists the **pairs of researchers who publish together**
without belonging to the same lab. It helps spot the bridges between labs and between poles — especially
**interdisciplinary** pairs — for the scientific coordination of a pole, a lab or the research office. It is read over
the period chosen at the top of the dashboard.

The pairs are computed on the **whole university corpus**, even from a lab or a pole: the Science and Technology pole
thus sees its pairs with the health, humanities and social science labs. The link **See the researcher pairs…** under
the diagram of the « Within the structure » and « Nantes Université » sub-tabs leads there too.

## Key figures

| Card | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Active pairs** | Pairs with at least one joint publication in the last three years of the period. |
| **New pairs** | Pairs whose first joint publication is from the last three years. |
| **Dormant pairs** | Pairs with nothing published together for three years. |
| **Between poles** | Pairs whose two researchers are in different poles. |
| **Distant profiles** | Pairs whose disciplinary profiles are distant (see below). |
| **Researchers of the structure in a pair** | How many researchers of the structure who published over the period have at least one pair, and their share. |
| **Researchers in a cross-pole pair** | The same for cross-pole pairs. |

## The two heatmaps

- **Pairs by couple of poles**: each cell counts the pairs between two poles; the diagonal counts the pairs of
  different labs within one pole.
- **Pairs by couple of labs**: the 20 most involved labs. **Click a cell** to list only the pairs of that couple of
  labs; click the label above the table (or the cell again) to come back to every pair.

A cell counts **pairs**, not publications: a very productive pair does not hide ten emerging ones.

## The table

For each pair: the two researchers (lab, pole), the number of **joint publications** over the period, the **share**
they represent in each researcher's output, the **trend** (new, rising, steady, dormant) with the years of the first
and last joint publication, the disciplinary **distance** and the main subfields of the joint publications. The
« Joint pubs », « Trend » and « Distance » columns sort with a click. **Click a row** to list the joint publications;
a researcher's name opens their record when they are identified in the directory. The **Pairs (CSV)** button downloads
the table shown.

## Filters

- **Which pairs**: researchers of different labs (default), of different poles, or a given couple of poles.
- **At least one researcher / both in the structure** (from a lab or a pole).
- **Membership**: every lab attribution, or only the labs where the directory lists the researcher (members and
  associates, or members only).
- **Minimum joint publications** (2 by default).
- **Distant disciplinary profiles only**, **Include PhD students**, **Exclude large collaborations**.

## Disciplinary distance

Each researcher has a profile: how all their publications spread over the OpenAlex fields (26 fields). The distance
is 0 for two identical profiles and 1 for two profiles without any common field. It is computed **outside the joint
publications**, otherwise a very productive pair would always look close. From 0.5 on, the pair has « distant
profiles ». Pole and distance complement each other: two dentistry researchers in labs of different poles have close
profiles, while two researchers of the same pole can be far apart.

## Researcher badges

- **associate**: the researcher is an associate or visiting member of the lab in the directory.
- **lab from publications**: the directory does not list them in this lab; the attribution comes from their
  publications. Former members, colleagues from other institutions, or affiliation errors (clinicians wrongly
  attributed to a lab): check before drawing conclusions.
- **PhD student**.

## In a report

The **Add to a report** button of both heatmaps puts them in one of your reports, with the filters of the sub-tab
(which pairs, membership, minimum joint publications…). In the report editor, the chart list (Collaborations section)
offers them too, and two blocks reuse their data: **Key figures — researcher pairs** and the **Researcher pairs**
table (most productive first). Their settings mirror the filters of the sub-tab; for a couple of poles, type the two pole
acronyms separated by `|`, for instance `Santé|S&T`.

These blocks do not follow the publication filters of the report (partner, country…): only the structure and the
period count. They have no public link: a shared report recomputes them with the rights of each reader.
## Suggested connections

The **Suggested connections** button, at the top of the sub-tab, lists researchers of **different labs who have not
co-signed any publication**, ranked by two signals computed over the period:

- **Common co-authors**: A and B both publish with C. A common co-author with few co-authors counts more than one who
  publishes with everyone (Adamic-Adar index). The three most specific common co-authors are named.
- **Shared subfields**: how close the OpenAlex subfields of their publications are, damped for researchers with few
  publications. The shared subfields are named.

The score is twice the common co-author signal plus the subfield one. Each researcher appears at most 5 times by
default, so that a very central researcher does not fill the list; PhD students are not suggested by default. The
pole, scope and membership filters are those of the observed pairs.

**What these suggestions are worth**: computed on 2020-2022, about one in six of the first 100 led to a joint
publication in 2023-2026, and one in fifteen between different poles. That is far above chance (a few pairs in
10,000), but they are **leads**: a suggestion not followed is not an error.

### Giving your opinion

Each suggestion carries three buttons: **Relevant**, **Already in contact**, **Not relevant**. One opinion per person
and pair, the last one wins; a second click on the same button withdraws it. The number next to a button counts the
opinions of every user. The suggestions you marked « Already in contact » or « Not relevant » leave the list; the
**Show the suggestions you set aside** box brings them back, greyed out. The opinion follows the two researchers from
one regeneration of the network to the next. With rights on the whole institution, the **Every opinion (CSV)** link
downloads every opinion, to judge the quality of the suggestions. Opinions are unavailable on instances without a
PostgreSQL database.

## Who sees what

- With rights on the **whole institution** or on a pole, every pair of the scope is visible.
- With rights limited to **your lab**, only the pairs with a researcher of your lab are listed.

## Known limits

- **The numbers are lower bounds**: about a quarter of the signatures are linked to no lab (abbreviated names in
  OpenAlex); the rate is shown under the table.
- **A researcher is identified by name**: two namesakes can be confused.
- **These figures describe joint publications**, not the performance of the researchers.
- The sub-tab does not exist on instances hosted on Cloudflare.
