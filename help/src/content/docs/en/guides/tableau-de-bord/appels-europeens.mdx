The **European calls** view (**Steering** family) brings the **topics** of the Horizon Europe
2026-2027 work programme together with the researchers of the structure, from their publications. It
does not replace the watch of the European affairs office: it points out where the work of a team
*appears* close to a topic, to talk it over.

## Where the matches come from

An automatic process (the *Topic-to-Researcher Match* batch of CRISalid) goes through the topics
cluster by cluster. For each topic, a language model writes queries, searches the closest
publications in the CRISalid graph of the institution, checks the candidates and writes a
**justification**. Druid imports the report of each cluster and joins it with its directory: only
the researchers known to Druid are shown, with a link to their record.

The banner at the top of the view says **which structures** the computation covered. When the
structure shown is outside that perimeter, a warning says so: its researchers only appear there as
co-authors of publications of the perimeter, which says nothing about its own coverage.

## The two views

| View | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Topics** | One topic per row: official title, identifier, destination, next deadline, number of researchers retained (including the **strong** matches). Expand the row to read the summary, the call, the deadlines, open the *Funding & Tenders* portal and see the researchers. |
| **Researchers** | One researcher per block, with the topics where they stand out. This is the view to show a researcher: "here is where your work appears". |

The filters apply to both views: free text search, **cluster** (CL1 health, CL2 culture and
society, CL4 digital and industry, CL5 climate and energy, CL6 food and environment…), **Include
closed calls** (by default only the topics with an upcoming deadline are listed) and, for the
topics, **Topics with researchers only**.

## Reading a match

Each match card carries:

- a **strength** assessed by the model from the publications: **strong**, **plausible** or
  **weak**;
- a **relative score bar**: the best candidate of the topic is at 100%, the others in proportion.
  It ranks the researchers *within a topic* and does not compare across topics: a candidate at 100%
  may still be judged weak;
- the **justification** written by the model (translated into French when the interface is in
  French);
- the **publications** that motivated the match (the first three, then "N more publications"),
  with the DOI link when Druid knows it, otherwise a search on OpenAlex.

## Giving an opinion

Under each match, three buttons: **Relevant**, **Not relevant**, **Already applying**. One opinion
per person and per match, the last one replaces the previous; the count of opinions shows next to
each button. These opinions serve to evaluate and improve the automatic process (they can be
exported for the CRISalid team): give one even on the obvious matches.

## Who sees what

- With a right on the **whole institution**, the matches of every structure.
- With a right limited to **your lab**, those of its researchers only.
- Every signed-in user can give an opinion; the export of the opinions is reserved to
  institution-wide rights.

## Known limits

- **Only the publications known to the CRISalid graph**: a researcher whose publications are not
  in it, or not yet vectorised, appears on no topic. The perimeter banner says which structures the
  computation covered.
- **One report per cluster, at a given date**: the topics of a cluster not imported are missing,
  and the matches are not recomputed when the directory or the publications change.
- **The deadlines come from the Funding & Tenders portal** at import time; check them on the
  portal before committing.
- **The perimeter is pillar 2** (clusters): no ERC grants, no Marie Skłodowska-Curie actions.
- Topic titles unknown to the portal keep the title extracted from the work programme, sometimes
  badly split.
