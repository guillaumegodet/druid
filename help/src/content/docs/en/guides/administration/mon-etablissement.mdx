The **My institution** tab gathers what concerns the Druid of your institution: its plan, its accounts and how
your colleagues get in.

## Plan and accounts

The top band shows the **plan** (Trial, Team, Complete), the **accounts** used out of those allowed and the depth of
the **publication history**. The trial includes one account, yours; the Team and Complete plans add more. An invited
account that has not signed in yet already counts.

## Move to a higher plan

The features your plan does not include stay visible, marked with a lock: **My reports**, the **Calls for
proposals** or **Benchmark** tabs of the dashboard. Opening them shows what they bring and the plan that includes
them. **Request a quote** (here, on those pages or in the « Trial » banner at the top of the screen) sends your request
to DirlaData: plan wanted, approximate number of researchers and accounts, message. Public institutions pay on invoice
with a purchase order; DirlaData activates the plan on receipt of the purchase order, with nothing for you to redo: your data, accounts and
settings stay in place.

## Invite a colleague

1. In **Members**, enter their e-mail address and choose their **role**:
   - **Owner**: everything, including the accounts and the plan;
   - **Administrator**: edits the directory, runs the alignments and the dashboard builds;
   - **Reader**: reads everything, edits nothing;
   - **Lab account**: reads and edits its labs only (enter their acronyms).
2. Click **Invite**. Your colleague receives an e-mail that opens the login page of your Druid, address already
   filled in: they ask for a sign-in link and are in.

Until they sign in, the row shows « Invited, never signed in » and **Resend** sends the invitation again. The role
menu changes the role of a member; the bin removes them and closes their sessions at once. The last owner of a Druid
can be neither demoted nor removed.

## Access requests

Enter the **mail domains** of the institution (for instance `univ-example.fr`). A colleague with an address of these
domains, without an account, who asks for a link on the login page sends you an **access request**: you receive an
e-mail and the request shows at the top of the tab. **Invite as reader** opens an account for them (you can change
the role afterwards), **Dismiss** files the request.

The login page always answers the same, account or not: it does not reveal who has an account.

## API keys of the institution

Some features go through services your institution has access to. The owner enters their keys in the **API keys of
the institution** card; administrators only see whether they are set.

- **Language model**: writes the AI texts of the reports and runs the thematic search of the collaborations. Any
  OpenAI-compatible service will do (ILaaS, Mistral, Albert, a model hosted by your IT department…): enter the
  **address of the API** (for example `https://api.mistral.ai/v1`), the **model** and the **key**.
- **Elsevier**: opens the alignment of the researchers on Scopus. An Elsevier key usually only works from the network
  of the subscribing institution; Druid calls it from outside, so it also needs the **institution token**
  (*insttoken*) Elsevier issues at the request of your library. A **backup key** takes over once the weekly quota of
  the first one is reached.

**Test and save** first tries the key (a short exchange with the service): if the test fails, nothing is saved and the
reason is shown (key refused, unknown model, missing institution token…). **Save anyway** keeps the key despite the
failure, for example when the service is briefly unavailable. The keys are stored encrypted and never shown again: to
change one, enter the new one; a key field left empty keeps the stored key. **Test again** runs the test again,
**Remove** removes the keys of the group.
