The **Watch** tab is for science communication: spotting what the structure has just
published, preparing a post or a newsletter, and following its researchers' presence in the
media.

## Spot recent publications

1. Open the **Watch** tab of the structure's dashboard.
2. Choose the **Analysis period** (the last 7, 30, 90… days). The data come **live from
   OpenAlex**, without waiting for the dashboard to be regenerated.
3. Filter by **Type**, **Journal type (quartile)**, **Keywords**, or **Open access
   publications only**.
4. Sort by **Most recent** or **Most cited**.
5. **Open the publication** leads to the publisher's page; **Export as CSV (filtered
   results)** downloads the list.

## Prepare a social media post

1. On a publication, click **Generate a LinkedIn / X post draft**.
2. The **Social media assistant** writes a text from the article's abstract.
3. Edit it, or **Generate another proposal**, then **Copy the text**.

## Put together a newsletter for the general public

1. Click **Newsletter**, then choose the **Newsletter issue (month)**.
2. **Generate briefs**: for each article of the last 30 days, the assistant writes a brief
   for the general public (a hook and 2-3 sentences).
3. Reread and edit each brief, then **Request validation** from the researcher concerned:
   Druid opens your mail client with the message ready.
4. Mark each brief **Validated** (or **Discard**) depending on the reply.
5. **Newsletter preview** shows the newsletter made of the validated briefs; **Download the
   HTML** to send it with your usual tool, then **Mark as published**.

## Follow media presence

The **Media presence** section lists the **Mentions** of the structure and its researchers
collected automatically (online press, videos, radio, podcasts): interview, expert quote,
op-ed, popular science… Communication officers validate or reject the detected mentions in
the **To review** tab (keyboard shortcuts shown on screen).

The sources monitored are set in [Manage media sources](/en/guides/veille/sources-medias/).

## What happens next

- Briefs and their status (generated, under validation, validated, published) are saved: an
  issue can be prepared over several days.
- Validated mentions stay visible in the tab; rejected ones do not come back.

## Known limitations

- **Only publications known to OpenAlex** appear, often a few days or weeks after they come
  out.
- **Generated texts are drafts**: always check them, and have the briefs validated by the
  researchers before sending them out.
- **Mention collection** only covers the sources followed; false positives (namesakes) are
  possible, hence the validation.
- **Writing assistants** depend on the instance.
